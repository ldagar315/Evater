## 1. Multiples and algebraic descriptions

### Think it through

A multiple of 7 can be written 7a, where a is an integer. Two such multiples add to 7a + 7b = 7(a + b). Because a + b is still an integer, the sum is another multiple of 7. This explains the pattern for every pair, rather than only a few examples.

### Try it yourself

Why is the sum of two multiples of 7 also a multiple of 7?

**Check:** 7a + 7b = 7(a + b), where a and b are integers.

## 2. Divisibility tests

### Watch: Divisibility Shortcuts: 1, 2, and 3

Professor Cunningham

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WC2puADmICM)

### Understand it

A divisibility test lets you check a number without completing the division. For divisibility by 3 or 9, add the digits and test that sum. For 47,232, the sum is 18, so the number is divisible by both 3 and 9. Use decimal place value to explain the rule, not just remember it.

### Try it yourself

Is 47,232 divisible by 9?

**Check:** Yes. Its digit sum is 18, a multiple of 9.

## 3. Combined divisibility conditions

### Watch: Least common multiple

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=znmPfDfsir8)

### Understand it

If a number must be divisible by two numbers, check both conditions. Their least common multiple gives the smallest positive number that meets both. For 4 and 6 it is 12, not 24. The video works with 18 and 12; the same prime-factor method finds the least common multiple of any pair. Multiplying the divisors can miss a smaller common multiple when they share factors.

### Try it yourself

Is every number divisible by 4 and 6 also divisible by 24?

**Check:** No. 12 is divisible by both but not by 24.

## 4. Digits in disguise

### Think it through

Treat a hidden digit as an unknown with only ten possible values: 0 to 9. In 42x, divisibility by 3 means 4 + 2 + x is divisible by 3. Try the allowed digits, then check the complete number. A restriction on the digit sum narrows the choices without guessing blindly.

### Try it yourself

Which digits can replace x in 42x if the number is divisible by 3?

**Check:** 0, 3, 6 or 9, since 4 + 2 + x must be divisible by 3.
