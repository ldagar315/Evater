## 1. Observations, questions and explanations

### Watch: Look Who's Talking: Crash Course Kids #27.2

Crash Course Kids

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=La0m_ocR7cQ)

### Understand it

An observation describes what you notice or measure. An explanation suggests why it happens. 'The plant bends toward the window' is an observation; 'the plant wants sunlight' adds a claim you have not tested. Turn the observation into a question you could investigate fairly. Plants convert light energy into chemical energy; they do not create energy.

### Try it yourself

A plant bends toward a window. Is "the plant wants sunlight" an observation?

**Check:** No. Bending toward the window is the observation; the explanation needs evidence.

## 2. Fair comparisons and variables

### Think it through

A fair comparison changes one relevant factor while keeping other relevant conditions comparable. To test dissolving at different temperatures, keep the substance, amount of water, solute mass, particle size and stirring method the same. Write what you change, what you measure and what you keep constant before starting.

### Try it yourself

To compare how temperature affects dissolving, what should stay the same?

**Check:** For example, the solute and its mass, water volume, stirring method and particle size.

## 3. Measuring, recording and patterns

### Watch: What's the difference between accuracy and precision? - Matt Anticole

Matt Anticole

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hRAFPdDppzs)

### Understand it

Write measurements with units and repeat them to check consistency. Accuracy concerns closeness to the correct value; precision concerns how closely repeated measurements agree. Repeated readings can cluster together yet still be wrong if the instrument or method has a systematic problem.

### Try it yourself

Why repeat a measurement?

**Check:** To check consistency and reduce the influence of random variation.

## 4. Evidence, conclusions and revising ideas

### Watch: Look Who's Talking: Crash Course Kids #27.2

Crash Course Kids

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=La0m_ocR7cQ)

### Understand it

A conclusion should answer your question using the results you collected. One successful trial does not prove a claim always holds. Keep an unexpected result in your record, check the method and explain what another trial could help you decide. Revising an explanation is part of doing science. When discussing the plant example, say that plants convert light energy into chemical energy, not that they make energy.

### Try it yourself

Does one successful trial prove a claim is always true?

**Check:** No. More evidence and checks may be needed, and the conclusion should state its limits.
