## 1. Reflection and mirror images

### Watch: Reflection in a plane mirror (1)

Cyberphysics

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QUZGek8pl5s)

### Understand it

A plane mirror reflects light: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. Measure both angles from the normal, a line perpendicular to the mirror. If the incident ray makes 35° with the normal, the reflected ray does too. Draw the normal before measuring any angle.

### Try it yourself

If the angle of incidence is 35°, what is the angle of reflection?

**Check:** 35°, measured from the normal.

## 2. Concave and convex mirrors

### Watch: Concave and Convex Mirrors

uclaphysicsvideo

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jtTBOMVMSYM)

### Understand it

Watch the image demonstrations only. Do not try focusing sunlight or making a heating experiment with a mirror. A concave mirror curves inward and a convex mirror curves outward. Their ray behaviour differs, and a concave mirror's image can change when you move the object. Do not memorise 'concave means magnified': sketch the mirror and label the object's position before discussing the image.

### Try it yourself

Does a concave mirror always give a magnified upright image?

**Check:** No. The image depends on object position.

## 3. Refraction and lenses

### Watch: How do glasses help us see? - Andrew Bastawrous and Clare Gilbert

Andrew Bastawrous and Clare Gilbert

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ypF037wlYZg)

### Understand it

Refraction changes the direction of light at an interface in the usual angled-ray cases. A lens primarily forms images by refraction, unlike a mirror. The video uses vision and glasses to show why directing light matters; keep that application separate from saying a lens works by reflection.

### Try it yourself

Does a lens form images only by reflecting light?

**Check:** No. A lens primarily forms images by refraction.

## 4. Image formation and safe observation

### Watch: Reflection in a plane mirror (1)

Cyberphysics

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QUZGek8pl5s)

### Understand it

The video shows how a plane-mirror image is constructed with rays; use the same careful-ray habit for any diagram here. Use a few well-chosen rays and label the object, optical surface and image. A clear diagram is more useful than many unlabeled lines. Never look at the Sun through a lens or magnifying glass, and never use one to focus sunlight as an unsupervised activity: it can cause severe eye injury or start a fire.

### Try it yourself

Is looking at the Sun through a magnifying glass safe?

**Check:** No. It can cause severe eye injury.
