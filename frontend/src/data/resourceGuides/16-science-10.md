## 1. Reflection and mirror images

### Watch: Reflection in a plane mirror (1)

Cyberphysics

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QUZGek8pl5s)

### Understand it

First draw the normal, a line perpendicular to the mirror at the point where the ray strikes. The angle of incidence and angle of reflection are both measured from this normal, not from the mirror surface. For a ray at 30° to the mirror surface, the angle of incidence is 90° - 30° = 60°. The reflected ray is also at 60° to the normal, or 30° to the surface.

This does not mean the two rays travel in the same direction. The incident ray points towards the mirror and the reflected ray points away, on the other side of the normal in the usual diagram. At normal incidence, the incident ray is along the normal: both angles are 0°, and the reflected ray returns along the same line.

### Try it yourself

An incident ray makes 25° with a plane mirror's surface. Find its angle of incidence and its angle of reflection. A student answers 25° for both. What reference line did the student use incorrectly?

**Check:** Both angles are 65°, since 90° - 25° = 65°. The student measured from the mirror surface instead of from the normal. The reflected ray makes 25° with the surface.

## 2. Concave and convex mirrors

### Watch: Concave and Convex Mirrors

uclaphysicsvideo

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jtTBOMVMSYM)

### Understand it

Watch the image demonstrations only. Do not try focusing sunlight or making a heating experiment with a mirror. A concave mirror curves inward; a convex mirror curves outward. For an ordinary object in front of a concave mirror, bringing it sufficiently close can give an upright, enlarged image. Moving it farther away can give an inverted image, whose size also depends on the distance. That is why "concave always means magnified and upright" is not a valid rule.

A convex mirror gives an upright, diminished image of an ordinary object in front of it, and can show a wide field of view. A plane mirror gives an upright image of the same size as the object. Use more than one observation when identifying a mirror: an upright image alone could come from any of these three. This is a comparison of image observations, not a task to focus sunlight or test mirrors on a road.

### Try it yourself

Three mirrors show these observations: A gives an upright image of the same size; B gives an upright, smaller image and a wide view; C gives an upright enlarged image nearby, but an inverted image farther away. Match A, B and C to plane, convex and concave mirrors. Why would "upright image" alone be insufficient?

**Check:** A is plane, B is convex, and C is concave. All three can show an upright image in the stated situations, so orientation alone cannot identify the mirror. Size and change with object position supply the extra evidence.

## 3. Refraction and lenses

### Watch: How do glasses help us see? - Andrew Bastawrous and Clare Gilbert

Andrew Bastawrous and Clare Gilbert

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ypF037wlYZg)

### Understand it

Light can change speed when it passes between transparent materials such as air and glass. When it enters at an angle to the normal, it usually changes direction too; this is the bending associated with refraction. Draw the normal perpendicular to the surface and measure angles from that line. A ray entering ordinary glass from air bends towards the normal. A transmitted ray returning from glass to air bends away from it.

Do not turn this into "every ray must bend". A ray entering straight along the normal can change speed without changing direction. A lens primarily forms images by refraction at its surfaces, whereas a mirror forms images by reflection. The vision video is an application of directing light, not a reason to say lenses work only by reflection. Never use a lens to look at the Sun or to focus sunlight.

### Try it yourself

Compare three transmitted rays: A enters glass from air at an angle to the normal; B enters along the normal; C leaves glass for air at a small nonzero angle. Which way does each bend? A pupil says B proves light has not changed speed because it did not bend. What is wrong with that conclusion?

**Check:** A bends towards the normal. B continues along the normal without bending, but its speed changes on entering glass. C bends away from the normal when transmitted into air. A direction change and a speed change are not the same test. Lens image formation primarily uses refraction, not just reflection.

## 4. Image formation and safe observation

### Watch: Reflection in a plane mirror (1)

Cyberphysics

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QUZGek8pl5s)

### Understand it

For a plane mirror, draw light rays from an object point to the mirror, then reflect them towards the observer using equal angles to the normal. Extend the reflected rays backwards with dashed lines. Their extensions meet behind the mirror at the apparent image point; actual reflected rays do not travel behind an ordinary opaque mirror.

The image is upright, the same size as the object and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front. It is virtual: you can see it, but cannot collect that image on a screen behind the mirror. For an object 12 cm in front, the image is 12 cm behind, so object-to-image separation is 24 cm. Use labelled ray diagrams or ordinary indoor observations only. Never look at the Sun through a lens or magnifying glass, or use lenses or mirrors to focus sunlight: severe eye injury or fire can result.

### Try it yourself

A small object starts 18 cm in front of a fixed plane mirror, then moves 5 cm towards it. Find the new image distance behind the mirror and the new object-to-image separation. By how much did the separation change? Could a screen placed behind the mirror catch the image?

**Check:** The object is now 18 - 5 = 13 cm in front, so the image is 13 cm behind. The separation is 13 + 13 = 26 cm, down from 36 cm: it decreased by 10 cm, twice the object's movement. A screen behind the mirror cannot catch this virtual image; the backward dashed lines are extensions, not actual light travelling there.
