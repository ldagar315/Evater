## 1. Conditions that support life

### Watch: Why extremophiles bode well for life beyond Earth - Louisa Preston

Louisa Preston

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bsp5JYNMAQE)

### Understand it

Life depends on interacting conditions, including liquid water, a suitable environment and energy. Distance from the Sun alone does not explain everything. The video explores organisms in extreme environments; use those examples to question simple assumptions, not as proof that life has been found on another planet.

### Try it yourself

Is distance from the Sun alone enough to explain all the conditions needed for life?

**Check:** No. Water, atmosphere and other interacting conditions also matter.

## 2. Earth systems and their connections

### Think it through

Land, water, air and living organisms affect one another. For example, vegetation can help hold soil with roots, and dead organic matter can contribute to it. Draw a chain linking a living thing to two non-living parts of its environment and explain each arrow.

### Try it yourself

How can vegetation affect soil?

**Check:** Roots can help hold soil and organic matter can contribute to it; living and non-living systems interact.

## 3. Continuity of life and reproduction

### Think it through

An individual can survive without reproducing, but reproduction enables populations or species to continue across generations. Keep those two questions separate: what helps this organism stay alive, and what allows new individuals to replace earlier generations? Use a familiar plant or animal to explain the difference.

### Try it yourself

Why does reproduction matter even though an individual can live without reproducing?

**Check:** It enables the continuation of the population or species across generations.

## 4. Threats and protection

### Watch: Maintaining biodiversity

Cognito

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bs9e6ovISbs)

Use the video to connect protecting species with protecting their habitats. It introduces conservation choices, not a wetland restoration procedure or a guarantee that one action removes every threat. Specific laws and farming choices depend on place and conditions. When considering a wetland, match each action to its pressure and check what remains unresolved. Do not catch, feed or disturb wildlife to test an idea.

### Understand it

Protecting life means protecting habitats and relationships, not only one familiar animal. Habitat change and pollution can affect many connected species. Choose one local example, trace the possible effects, and distinguish a helpful individual action from the wider changes needed to protect the system.

### Try it yourself

Why is protecting only one familiar animal not the whole of conservation?

**Check:** Species depend on habitats and wider ecological relationships, so connected systems also need protection.
