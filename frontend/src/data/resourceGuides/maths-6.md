## 1. The distributive property

### Watch: Expanding Brackets - Corbettmaths

corbettmaths

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZJA1qz4XovY)

### Understand it

To multiply a whole group, multiply every part: a(b + c) = ab + ac. For 17 × 98, use 98 = 100 - 2. Then 17 × 98 = 1,700 - 34 = 1,666. A split rectangle can show the same calculation as two areas.

### Try it yourself

Calculate 17 × 98 without long multiplication.

**Check:** 17 × (100 - 2) = 1700 - 34 = 1666.

## 2. Products of sums and differences

### Watch: Multiplying binomials intro

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xy8NKUoyy98)

### Understand it

Expand (2x + 3)(x - 4) by multiplying every term in one bracket by every term in the other. Keep all four contributions visible:

- 2x × x = 2x²
- 2x × (-4) = -8x
- 3 × x = 3x
- 3 × (-4) = -12

Add the entries: 2x² - 8x + 3x - 12 = 2x² - 5x - 12. The negative sign belongs to -4, including when it multiplies 3. At x = 5, the brackets give 13 × 1 = 13 and the expansion gives 50 - 25 - 12 = 13. This checks one value; multiplying every term explains the identity for every x.

### Try it yourself

Expand (3y - 2)(y + 5). Then evaluate the original and expanded expressions at y = -1. Explain why the constant term is negative.

**Check:** 3y² + 13y - 10. At y = -1, both give -20. The constant is (-2) × 5 = -10, not +10.

## 3. Squares and difference of squares

### Watch: Squaring a binomial

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4GcNzvILqtM)

### Understand it

Squaring a sum means multiplying the whole bracket by itself. (a + b)² gives a² + ab + ab + b² = a² + 2ab + b². The two cross-products explain the middle term. For a difference of squares, (a + b)(a - b) = a² - b² because the cross-products cancel.

### Try it yourself

Why is (x + 4)² not x² + 16?

**Check:** It also includes 8x: the two cross-products are 4x and 4x.

## 4. Finding and fixing expansion errors

### Watch: Keep signs when expanding brackets

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3NHSwiv_pSE)

Follow each signed multiplier before collecting like terms. A negative multiplier acts on every term inside its bracket. Then use the correction below to explain the error, not only to replace the answer. A numerical counterexample can disprove an identity; agreement at one value does not prove it.

### Understand it

Consider 4(2t - 3) - 2(t + 5). A proposed answer 6t - 2 has lost the negative multiplier on 5. Distribute first, keeping each sign: 8t - 12 - 2t - 10 = 6t - 22. At t = 0, the original gives -12 - 10 = -22, so the proposed answer -2 is already disproved. A counterexample exposes an error; the correct distributive steps explain the repair.

Beware of a check that passes accidentally. The wrong claim (u + 2)² = u² + 4 is true at u = 0, but at u = 1 its sides are 9 and 5. The missing term is 4u. Matching at one value does not prove an identity.

### Try it yourself

A student writes -3(2p - 5) + 4p = -2p - 15. Identify the incorrect product, repair the answer, and use p = 2 to check both sides.

**Check:** (-3) × (-5) is +15, not -15. The result is -2p + 15. At p = 2, the original and corrected expression both give 11; the student's answer gives -19.
