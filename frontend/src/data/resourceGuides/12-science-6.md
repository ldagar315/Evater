## 1. Pressure and contact area

### Watch: GCSE Physics - Pressure - Equation & How It Works

Cognito

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sfnsuS0-o58)

### Understand it

Pressure = perpendicular force ÷ contact area. Compare a 60 N force spread over 0.30 m² with the same force over 0.10 m². The pressures are 60/0.30 = 200 Pa and 60/0.10 = 600 Pa. The force has not increased: reducing the area to one-third has made the pressure three times as large. A wider bag strap can reduce pressure without reducing the bag's weight.

Do not compare areas alone when the forces also change. A 120 N force on 0.60 m² gives 200 Pa, the same pressure as 60 N on 0.30 m². Doubling both force and area leaves their ratio unchanged. Use force in newtons and area in square metres to obtain pascals.

### Try it yourself

Two pads support separate loads. Pad A has 90 N acting perpendicularly over 0.30 m²; pad B has 120 N over 0.60 m². Which pad has greater pressure? Does the greater force necessarily mean greater pressure?

**Check:** A: 90/0.30 = 300 Pa. B: 120/0.60 = 200 Pa. A has the greater pressure despite the smaller force. Both force and area matter.

## 2. Air pressure and winds

### Watch: How heavy is air? - Dan Quinn

Dan Quinn

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VDf00z8sMFw)

### Understand it

Air exerts pressure even when there is no noticeable wind. Pressure differences can drive air movement. In a simple warming diagram, show rising warm air and replacement flow, rather than saying the rising air leaves a vacuum. The video explores air's weight; connect it to the idea that air is matter.

### Try it yourself

Does air exert pressure even when you cannot feel a strong wind?

**Check:** Yes. Air pressure exists without a noticeable wind.

## 3. Storms, thunder and lightning

### Watch: The science of static electricity - Anuradha Bhagwat

TED-Ed

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yc2-363MIQs)

### Understand it

Lightning is an electrical discharge; static electricity is a useful starting point for its explanation. A school model is not storm-safety advice. During a storm, follow a responsible adult and official guidance: seek substantial shelter, not an isolated tall tree. Do not try to observe lightning from an exposed place.

### Try it yourself

Is sheltering under an isolated tall tree during lightning a safe choice?

**Check:** No. Seek a substantial building or other officially recommended safe shelter.

## 4. Cyclones and reducing risk

### Think it through

Connect warm moist air, pressure differences and circulation when describing cyclones. For real risk, forecasts and official warnings matter more than what you can see outside. Follow evacuation instructions from local authorities in time; do not wait until a cyclone is visible.

### Try it yourself

Should you wait to see a cyclone before following an evacuation instruction?

**Check:** No. Follow official warnings and local authorities in time.
