## 1. Angle sums in quadrilaterals

### Watch: Angles in a Quadrilateral

corbettmaths

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9udtA3cPgU)

### Understand it

A diagonal splits a quadrilateral into two triangles. Each triangle has an angle sum of 180°, so the quadrilateral's interior angles total 360°. Add the known angles and subtract from 360° to find the missing one. Do not decide from how wide an angle looks in the drawing.

### Try it yourself

Three angles are 80°, 95° and 105°. Find the fourth.

**Check:** 80°, because 360 - 80 - 95 - 105 = 80.

## 2. Parallelograms and diagonals

### Watch: Why parallelogram diagonals bisect each other

Kevinmathscience

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PC9mbAwE3zk)

Follow the two opposite triangles in the proof. The video uses two angles and a corresponding non-included side, often called AAS or SAA. It proves that each diagonal is cut into two equal halves, not that the two whole diagonals are equal or perpendicular. It does not prove the converse. Then return to the original explanation and question below.

### Understand it

In a parallelogram, opposite sides are parallel and the diagonals bisect each other: each cuts the other into two equal parts. That does not mean the two diagonals have equal lengths. Draw a slanted parallelogram and label the halves of each diagonal separately.

### Try it yourself

Must the diagonals of every parallelogram have equal length?

**Check:** No. They bisect each other, but need not have equal length.

## 3. Rectangles, rhombuses and squares

### Watch: Quadrilateral properties | Perimeter, area, and volume | Geometry | Khan Academy

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5CeBlu260Rw)

### Understand it

A rectangle has four right angles; a rhombus has four equal sides. A square has both sets of properties, so it belongs to both groups. A rectangle need not have four equal sides, and a rhombus need not have four right angles. Test a classification by its properties, not its orientation.

### Try it yourself

Is every rectangle a square?

**Check:** No. A rectangle needs four right angles, but its four sides need not be equal.

## 4. Kites, trapeziums and constructions

### Think it through

Before drawing a kite or trapezium, mark the lengths, angles and parallel sides you are given. Trapezium definitions vary, so use the convention stated in your class. A neat-looking sketch does not prove parallelism: explain it using the given conditions or a valid angle argument.

### Try it yourself

Why is a sketch alone not proof that two sides are parallel?

**Check:** A sketch can be distorted. Parallelism needs given information, a construction condition or a valid angle argument.
