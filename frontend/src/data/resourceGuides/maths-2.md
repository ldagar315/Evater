## 2. Multiplying, dividing and raising powers

Use the structure of the product to justify each exponent law. The same-base condition matters.

### Why powers of powers multiply exponents

Original worked explanation, not a video.

Expand (2³)² as (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2). There are six factors of 2, so (2³)² = 2⁶ = 64. The outer exponent counts groups; the inner exponent counts factors per group.

For positive integer exponents m and n, (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ. Do not add m and n: 2³ × 2² = 2⁵ is a different expression. If a product is inside the brackets, raise every factor: (3a²)² = 3² × (a²)² = 9a⁴.

[Read the supporting source](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/hegp102.pdf)

This original worked explanation covers positive integer outer powers. The written RMIT resource also includes negative-power examples, which need the nonzero-base condition.

New-step question: Compare (5²)³ with 5² × 5³. Are they equal?

New-step answer: (5²)³ = 5⁶ = 15625. But 5² × 5³ = 5⁵ = 3125. Multiplying powers and raising a power are different operations.

## 4. Powers of ten and scientific notation

Connect decimal-point movement to place value. In standard scientific notation, keep the leading number at least 1 and less than 10.

### Convert and check scientific notation

Original worked explanation, not a video.

For the positive numbers used here, scientific notation has the form c × 10ᵏ with 1 ≤ c < 10. For 72,000, choose 7.2 and move the decimal four places right to recover the original number: 72,000 = 7.2 × 10⁴.

For 0.00072, choose the same 7.2 but make the value smaller: 0.00072 = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴. Check by dividing 7.2 by 10,000. If an intermediate answer is 72 × 10⁻⁵, it has the right value but is not standard: divide 72 by 10 and increase the exponent by 1, giving 7.2 × 10⁻⁴.

[Read the supporting source](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/hegp102.pdf)

The linked 4:12 clip works one small positive decimal; the large-number and normalisation examples are original written follow-ups, not attributed to that clip.

New-step question: Write 0.000045 in scientific notation and normalise 45 × 10⁴.

New-step answer: 0.000045 = 4.5 × 10⁻⁵. Also 45 × 10⁴ = 4.5 × 10⁵. In each case the coefficient is at least 1 and less than 10.
