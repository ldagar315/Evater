# Class 8 Maths: Power Play - Watch, Understand and Practise

Pick one topic. Watch the video, explain the idea aloud, then try the question without looking at the answer.

## 1. What does a power mean?

### Watch: Introduction to exponents

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XZRQhkii0h0)

### Understand it

A power is a short way to write repeated multiplication. In 3⁴, the base 3 tells you what to multiply; the exponent 4 tells you how many factors to use. So 3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81, not 3 × 4.

### Try it yourself

Write 2⁵ as a product and find its value. Then explain why 2⁵ and 5² are different.

**Check:** 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32. But 5² = 25. Swapping the base and exponent changes the meaning.

## 2. Multiply, divide and raise powers

### Watch: Multiplying and dividing powers

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CZ5ne_mX5_I)

### Understand it

For the same base, add exponents when multiplying and subtract them when dividing: 2⁵ × 2³ ÷ 2⁴ = 2⁴ = 16. You can check this by expanding the factors and cancelling. The base must be nonzero when you divide or use negative exponents.

Now take one more step: a power of a power is different. (2³)² means two groups of three factors of 2, so it is 2⁶, not 2⁵. Multiply the exponents: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ for positive integer exponents.

### Try it yourself

Compare (5²)³ with 5² × 5³. Before calculating, say whether you are raising a power or multiplying two powers.

**Check:** (5²)³ = 5⁶ = 15,625. But 5² × 5³ = 5⁵ = 3,125. For revision, write one example of each operation and explain the rule without looking.

## 3. Zero and negative exponents

### Watch: Negative exponent intuition

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tqpcku0hrPU)

### Understand it

Follow the pattern 5² = 25, 5¹ = 5, 5⁰ = 1. Each step divides by 5. Keep going: 5⁻¹ = 1/5 and 5⁻² = 1/25. A negative exponent means a reciprocal, not a negative answer. These rules need a nonzero base.

### Try it yourself

Continue the pattern 2², 2¹, 2⁰, 2⁻¹, 2⁻². What stays the same between neighbouring terms?

**Check:** 4, 2, 1, 1/2, 1/4. Each term is the previous term divided by 2. Cover the list and rebuild it tomorrow instead of memorising five separate facts.

## 4. Scientific notation

### Watch: Writing a small number in scientific notation

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/embed/6phoVfGKKec)

### Understand it

The video works through a small decimal. For a positive number in standard scientific notation, write c × 10ᵏ with 1 ≤ c < 10. For example, 72,000 = 7.2 × 10⁴, while 0.00072 = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴. Check by multiplying or dividing by the power of ten, not by guessing the sign.

A form such as 72 × 10⁻⁵ has the right value but is not standard. Divide 72 by 10 and increase the exponent by 1: 7.2 × 10⁻⁴.

### Try it yourself

Write 0.000045 in scientific notation. Then rewrite 45 × 10⁴ in standard form.

**Check:** 4.5 × 10⁻⁵ and 4.5 × 10⁵. Multiply each answer back out to check it.
