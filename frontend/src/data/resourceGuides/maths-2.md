# Class 8 Maths: Power Play - Watch, Understand and Practise

Pick one topic. Watch the video, explain the idea aloud, then try the question without looking at the answer.

## 1. What does a power mean?

### Watch: Introduction to exponents

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XZRQhkii0h0)

### Understand it

A power counts equal factors, not additions. In 3⁴, the base 3 is the repeated factor and the exponent 4 counts four copies: 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81. It does not mean 3 × 4. Swapping the roles gives 4³ = 64, a different product. Increasing an exponent by one multiplies by the base; it does not add the base.

Brackets also tell you the base. In (-2)² the repeated factor is -2, giving (-2) × (-2) = 4. In -2², the minus is outside the power: -(2 × 2) = -4. Read the base and exponent before calculating.

### Try it yourself

A fictional label has three positions. Each position independently allows A or B, and letters may repeat. How many labels are possible? A pupil says that allowing C as well must triple that answer. Test the claim. Separately, what happens if you keep A/B but add a fourth position?

**Check:** There are 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 labels. With A/B/C at every position there are 3 × 3 × 3 = 27, not 3 × 8 = 24. The extra choice affects all three positions, not just one. With A/B and four positions there are 2⁴ = 16: adding one position doubles the count. Changing the number of choices and changing the number of positions are different operations.

## 2. Multiply, divide and raise powers

### Watch: Multiplying and dividing powers

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CZ5ne_mX5_I)

### Understand it

For the same base, multiplication joins lists of factors: 2³ × 2² has five factors of 2, so it is 2⁵. Division cancels matching factors: 2⁵ ÷ 2² = 2³. The divisor must not be zero. These rules do not say to add the bases, and they do not turn a sum such as 2³ + 2² into 2⁵.

A power of a power makes repeated groups instead. (2³)² = (2 × 2 × 2) × (2 × 2 × 2) = 2⁶. Multiply the exponents here. Compare the operation signs first: a product of powers adds exponents, a quotient subtracts them, and a power of a power multiplies them. For quotients and negative exponents, use a nonzero base.

### Try it yourself

A pupil writes (3²)³ ÷ 3⁴ = 3¹ because they add 2 + 3 and then subtract 4. Find the actual result by grouping factors. Then decide whether 3² + 3³ = 3⁵, using the numerical values rather than a memorised rule.

**Check:** The bracket is raised to a power: (3²)³ has 2 × 3 = 6 factors of 3. Cancelling four gives 3² = 9, not 3¹. Also 3² + 3³ = 9 + 27 = 36, whereas 3⁵ = 243. Adding exponents applies to multiplying powers with the same base, not to adding their values. The two mistakes come from ignoring different operation signs.

## 3. Zero and negative exponents

### Watch: Negative exponent intuition

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tqpcku0hrPU)

### Understand it

To extend powers consistently, divide by the nonzero base whenever the exponent falls by one: 5² = 25, 5¹ = 5, 5⁰ = 1, 5⁻¹ = 1/5 and 5⁻² = 1/25. The zero exponent is not multiplication by zero. A negative exponent means take the reciprocal of a positive power, not make the answer negative.

For example, 3⁻² = 1/(3 × 3) = 1/9, while -3² = -9. Brackets can change the base: (-3)⁻² = 1/[(-3) × (-3)] = 1/9. These zero and negative exponent rules require a nonzero base; they do not define 0⁰ or 0⁻¹.

### Try it yourself

A pupil says 2⁻³ = -8. Repair the answer and check your repair by multiplying it by 2³. Then find (-2)⁻³ and 7⁰. Explain why the same rule cannot give a value for 0⁻².

**Check:** 2⁻³ = 1/8, and (1/8) × 8 = 1 checks the reciprocal. But (-2)⁻³ = 1/[(-2) × (-2) × (-2)] = -1/8: an odd number of negative factors makes the denominator negative. Also 7⁰ = 1, not 0. Finally, 0⁻² would require 1/0² = 1/0, which is undefined. A negative exponent alone does not determine the sign; the base and exponent both matter.

## 4. Scientific notation

### Watch: Writing a small number in scientific notation

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/embed/6phoVfGKKec)

### Understand it

For a positive number in standard scientific notation, write c × 10ᵏ with 1 ≤ c < 10 and integer k. Thus 72,000 = 7.2 × 10⁴, while 0.00072 = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴. The power of ten restores the size after you choose the coefficient. Multiplying back out is a better check than guessing the sign.

Equivalent values need not be standard: 72 × 10⁻⁵ has coefficient 72, outside the allowed interval. Dividing that coefficient by 10 must be balanced by multiplying the power-of-ten factor by 10, giving 7.2 × 10⁻⁴. A negative exponent here describes a small positive number, not a negative measurement.

### Try it yourself

Two fictional measured lengths are A = 0.000084 m and B = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ m. Write A in standard scientific notation. A pupil writes it as 84 × 10⁻⁶ m: is that the wrong value, nonstandard notation, or both? Which length is larger, and by what factor?

**Check:** A = 8.4 × 10⁻⁵ m. Multiplying 84 × 10⁻⁶ gives the same 0.000084, so its value is correct but its coefficient makes it nonstandard. Both standard forms now have exponent -5: compare 8.4 with 6.3. A is larger, and A/B = 8.4/6.3 = 4/3. Both lengths are positive; the minus signs belong to the exponents. Matching powers of ten makes the comparison transparent.
