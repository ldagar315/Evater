## 1. Rotation, revolution and time

### Watch: Earth's rotation and revolution

Crash Course Kids

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l64YwNl1wr0)

### Understand it

Earth's rotation is its spin and gives the day-night cycle. Revolution is its motion around the Sun and is linked to the year. Draw separate arrows for those motions. A month is linked to the Moon's cycle; do not use one motion to explain all three time intervals.

### Try it yourself

Which motion gives Earth its day-night cycle?

**Check:** Earth's rotation.

## 2. Moon phases

### Watch: What causes the phases of the moon?

Veritasium

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jip3BbZBpsM)

### Understand it

The Sun lights half the Moon. As the Moon orbits Earth, we see different amounts of that sunlit half, giving the ordinary phases. A crescent is not normally Earth's shadow falling on the Moon. Draw the Sun, Earth and Moon, then mark the illuminated half before shading the view from Earth.

### Try it yourself

Is a crescent Moon normally a partial lunar eclipse?

**Check:** No. Phases are the changing view of the Moon's sunlit half.

## 3. Lunar and solar calendars

### Think it through

A lunar month follows the Moon's phase cycle, while a solar year follows Earth's annual cycle around the Sun. Twelve lunar months are shorter than a solar year. Without adjustments, a calendar based only on those months shifts relative to the seasons. Ask what each calendar is trying to keep aligned.

### Try it yourself

Why can a calendar based only on lunar months drift relative to the seasons?

**Check:** Twelve lunar months are shorter than a solar year.

## 4. Leap years and calendar adjustment

### Think it through

A calendar has to use whole days, while the seasonal year is not exactly 365 days. Adding a leap day roughly every four years helps, but that alone adds too many days over long periods. The Gregorian rule corrects this with century exceptions.

Use this decision chain for a Gregorian year: if it is not divisible by 4, it is not a leap year. If it is divisible by 4 but not by 100, it is a leap year. If it is divisible by 100, it is a leap year only if it is also divisible by 400. For example, 2028 passes the ordinary four-year rule; 1900 fails the century exception; 2000 passes the 400-year exception. A Gregorian leap year has 366 days, including 29 February. Other calendars need their own rules.

### Try it yourself

A student says 2100 and 2400 must both be Gregorian leap years because both divide by 4. Check that claim, then decide whether 2027 and 2032 are leap years. Explain which test settles each case.

**Check:** 2100 is not a leap year: divisible by 100 but not 400. 2400 is a leap year: divisible by 400. 2027 is not divisible by 4, so is not a leap year. 2032 is divisible by 4 but not 100, so is a leap year.
