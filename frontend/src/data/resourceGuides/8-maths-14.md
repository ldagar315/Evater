## 1. Area, unit squares and rectangles

### Think it through

Area counts the equal square units that cover a region without gaps or overlaps. A 7 cm by 4 cm rectangle holds 7 × 4 = 28 one-centimetre squares. Write 28 cm², not 28 cm: the measurement describes a surface, not a length.

### Try it yourself

Find the area of a 7 cm by 4 cm rectangle.

**Check:** 28 cm², not 28 cm.

## 2. Parallelograms and perpendicular height

### Watch: Area of parallelograms intuition

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hm17lVaor0Q)

### Understand it

Cutting a triangle off one end of a parallelogram and moving it to the other can make a rectangle with the same area. The rectangle's dimensions are the base and perpendicular height. For base 8 cm and height 3 cm, the area is 24 cm², even if the sloping side is 5 cm.

### Try it yourself

A parallelogram has base 8 cm, perpendicular height 3 cm and sloping side 5 cm. Find its area.

**Check:** 24 cm². The sloping side is not the height.

## 3. Triangles and quadrilaterals

### Watch: Finding area by breaking up the shape

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=loAA3TCNAvU)

### Understand it

A triangle is half a parallelogram with the same base and perpendicular height, so its area is ½ × base × height. For a less familiar region, draw dividing lines and add the areas of non-overlapping pieces. Keep the units the same before combining them.

### Try it yourself

Find the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and height 6 cm.

**Check:** 30 cm².

## 4. Trapeziums, rhombuses and composite regions

### Watch: Finding area by breaking up the shape

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=loAA3TCNAvU)

### Understand it

For a trapezium, add the parallel sides, multiply by the perpendicular height, then halve. For a rhombus with perpendicular diagonals, use half their product. For a region with a hole, find the outside area and subtract the missing area. Check which lengths your diagram actually gives before choosing a formula.

### Try it yourself

A trapezium has parallel sides 6 cm and 10 cm with height 4 cm. Find its area.

**Check:** 32 cm², using half the sum of the parallel sides times the height.
