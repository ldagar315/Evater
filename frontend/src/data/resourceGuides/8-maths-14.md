## 1. Area, unit squares and rectangles

### Think it through

Area counts the equal square units that cover a region without gaps or overlaps. A 7 cm by 4 cm rectangle holds 7 × 4 = 28 one-centimetre squares. Write 28 cm², not 28 cm: the measurement describes a surface, not a length.

### Try it yourself

Find the area of a 7 cm by 4 cm rectangle.

**Check:** 28 cm², not 28 cm.

## 2. Parallelograms and perpendicular height

### Watch: Area of parallelograms intuition

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hm17lVaor0Q)

### Understand it

A parallelogram can be cut and rearranged into a rectangle without adding or losing area. The rectangle uses the chosen base and the perpendicular distance to the opposite parallel side. For base 9 cm and height 4 cm, the area is 36 cm², even if the sloping side is 5 cm. That sloping side is a length along the boundary, not the perpendicular height.

You may choose another side as the base, but must use its matching height. Taking the 5 cm side as base gives height 36/5 = 7.2 cm. Both base-height pairs describe the same area. Sliding the top edge sideways while keeping base and perpendicular height fixed changes the slant, not the area.

### Try it yourself

A parallelogram has base 12 cm, perpendicular height 5 cm and sloping side 13 cm. Find its area. If the 13 cm side is chosen as base instead, what is its corresponding height? Explain why multiplying 12 by 13 is wrong.

**Check:** The area is 12 × 5 = 60 cm². The height for the 13 cm base is 60/13 cm, about 4.62 cm. Multiplying 12 × 13 uses two side lengths, not a base and its perpendicular height; the sloping side is not the given 5 cm height.

## 3. Triangles and quadrilaterals

### Watch: Finding area by breaking up the shape

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=loAA3TCNAvU)

### Understand it

A triangle has half the area of a parallelogram with the same base and perpendicular height: A = ½bh. For base 10 cm and height 7 cm, A = 35 cm². The perpendicular may meet an extension of the base in an obtuse triangle; it still measures the distance to the base line, not the sloping side.

A diagonal can divide a convex quadrilateral into two non-overlapping triangles. If that diagonal is 12 cm long and the two opposite vertices are 4 cm and 3 cm from its line, the total area is ½ × 12 × 4 + ½ × 12 × 3 = 42 cm². Add the triangle areas once each; the shared diagonal has no area to add.

### Try it yourself

Find the area of a triangle with base 11 cm and perpendicular height 8 cm. Then find the area of a convex quadrilateral with diagonal AC = 16 cm, where B and D are on opposite sides of AC at perpendicular distances 3 cm and 5 cm. If B moves parallel to AC without changing that distance or losing convexity, does the area change?

**Check:** The triangle has area ½ × 11 × 8 = 44 cm². The quadrilateral has area ½ × 16 × (3 + 5) = 64 cm². Moving B as stated keeps the common base and its perpendicular height fixed, so both triangle areas, and hence the total, stay unchanged.

## 4. Trapeziums, rhombuses and composite regions

### Watch: Finding area by breaking up the shape

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=loAA3TCNAvU)

### Understand it

Choose a formula from the shape's properties, not from any two lengths you see. Two identical trapeziums fit into a parallelogram, giving A = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × perpendicular height. Parallel sides 8 cm and 14 cm with height 5 cm give 55 cm².

A rhombus has perpendicular diagonals, so its four right-triangle pieces give half the product of the diagonals. Diagonals 10 cm and 16 cm give 80 cm². This shortcut does not apply to an arbitrary quadrilateral whose diagonals are not perpendicular. For a composite plate or region with a hole, subtract the missing area: a 10 cm by 8 cm rectangle minus a 3 cm by 2 cm hole leaves 74 cm². Keep all lengths in compatible units first.

### Try it yourself

Calculate separately: a trapezium with parallel sides 9 cm and 15 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm; a rhombus with diagonals 8 cm and 12 cm; and a 15 cm by 8 cm rectangular plate with a 3 cm by 4 cm rectangular hole. Explain why equal answers for the first two do not make the shapes congruent.

**Check:** The trapezium area is ½ × (9 + 15) × 4 = 48 cm². The rhombus area is ½ × 8 × 12 = 48 cm². The plate has area 15 × 8 - 3 × 4 = 108 cm². Equal area measures the same amount of surface, not identical side lengths, angles or shape, so it does not imply congruence.
