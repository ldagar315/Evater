## 1. Mean as fair share or balance

### Watch: Mean | Data sets and distribution | 6th grade math (Illustrative Math-aligned) | Khan Academy

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_40su2dEvA4)

### Understand it

The mean is the amount each entry would have after sharing the total equally. For 4, 6, 8 and 10, add to get 28, then divide by the four entries: 7. The mean need not be one of the original values. A fair-share drawing helps explain the calculation.

### Try it yourself

Find the mean of 4, 6, 8 and 10.

**Check:** 7, because their sum 28 is shared among four values.

## 2. Reading graphs and axes

### Watch: Misleading line graphs

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gs-OPF3KEGU)

### Understand it

Read a graph's title, axis labels, units and scale before comparing heights. If each grid step is 5 units, three steps mean 15 units. Changing an axis scale can make a small difference look large, so describe the numerical change rather than only the steepness or bar height.

### Try it yourself

A graph axis advances by 5 each grid step. What does three steps represent?

**Check:** 15 units, not 3.

## 3. Line graphs and change over time

### Watch: Reading line graphs

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=36v2EXZRzUE)

### Understand it

A line graph shows how a quantity changes as you move along the horizontal axis, often time. Read two labelled points before calculating a change. The video tracks monthly stock prices; read any line graph the same way, whatever it measures. A flat segment means the vertical-axis reading stays constant over that interval; it does not automatically mean the reading is zero.

### Try it yourself

Does a flat distance-time line mean the object has returned to its starting point?

**Check:** No. It means its distance reading is not changing during that interval.

## 4. Choosing and interpreting a display

### Watch: Misleading line graphs

Khan Academy

[Open the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gs-OPF3KEGU)

### Understand it

Choose a display to match the question: a line graph can show change over time, while other displays suit other comparisons. Check the scale and labels whichever display you use. If two quantities rise together, that is an association, not proof that one caused the other.

### Try it yourself

Can a graph showing two quantities rising together prove one causes the other?

**Check:** No. Other explanations may exist.
